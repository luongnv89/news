OpenAI launches ChatGPT Wallet so its dots agents can pay for purchases, starting with US Pro subscribers

Published: 13 Nov 2026, 16:00 CET

OpenAI introduced ChatGPT Wallet on Friday afternoon, a way for its dots agents to complete purchases for a user. It was built with American Express, Mastercard and Visa and starts rolling out to ChatGPT Pro subscribers in the US aged 18 and over with eligible US-issued cards.

Users add a card once. Before each purchase they review the items, the total and the card, then approve it with a security check such as Face ID or a fingerprint. The payment network then issues a payment token in place of the real card number, so neither the agent nor the shop sees the card details, and card rewards and dispute protections still apply, OpenAI says.

OpenAI says it is the first wallet that lets agents pay without access to users' actual card details, which is a vendor claim. It also published a Commerce Partner Playbook telling businesses how to let ChatGPT agents through their firewalls, using signed Web Bot Auth requests, and how to reduce declined agent payments. Later, users should be able to pre-approve purchases within limits such as spending caps or categories, the company says.

TechCrunch notes the limited launch lets OpenAI test the feature before a wider rollout, and that some large retailers, Amazon among them, block AI agents from their sites.

Analysis — why it matters. Analysis: payments are the step where shopping agents have stalled. OpenAI is betting that tokenised cards and a per-purchase biometric check will satisfy banks and shops; whether merchants stop blocking agents is the open question.

Caveat. The security and first-of-its-kind statements are OpenAI's own. Availability is limited to US Pro subscribers at launch.

LangChain raises $300 million at a $3 billion valuation, led by Alphabet's CapitalG

Published: 13 Nov 2026

LangChain, which makes tools for building and running AI agents, announced on Friday a $300 million Series C at a $3 billion valuation, TechCrunch reports. CapitalG led the round, with GIC, DFJ Growth, IVP and Sapphire Ventures taking part.

The valuation is more than double the $1.25 billion of its $125 million round about a year ago. The company pitches its LangSmith platform as a way to build and manage agents without being locked into one model or cloud, and claims 7,000 LangSmith customers, including Nvidia, Toyota and Salesforce.

Analysis — why it matters. Analysis: investors are still paying up for model-neutral agent tooling, even as the frontier labs ship their own agent platforms.

Caveat. The customer count and positioning are the company's claims, as reported by TechCrunch.

Google lets all US Gemini app users connect Google Wallet for answers based on their spending

Published: 13 Nov 2026, 18:00 CET

Google added Google Wallet to Gemini's connected apps on Friday, rolling out to all Gemini app users in the US on the web, Android and iOS. Users can also link bank and credit-card accounts to Wallet through Plaid for deeper insights.

Tagging @Google Wallet lets Gemini chart spending, find recurring subscriptions and their price rises, or suggest a holiday budget from last year's transactions. Google says the connection is opt-in and read-only: Gemini cannot move, manage or invest money.

Analysis — why it matters. Analysis: on the same afternoon OpenAI let its agents pay, Google took the more cautious route of letting its assistant read, but not spend.

OpenAI breaks up its stand-alone robotics team and moves robotics research into its main research group, Fortune reports

Published: 13 Nov 2026, 18:05 CET

OpenAI has disbanded its stand-alone robotics team and reassigned its researchers, Fortune reported on Friday; the company confirmed the reorganisation. Robotics research now sits in the main research team under chief research officer Mark Chen, while applied robotics and hardware move to the Applied organisation under president Greg Brockman.

A spokesperson said robotics remains a core part of OpenAI's long-term vision and presented the move as a commitment rather than a retreat. The company told Fortune it now wants its general-purpose models to control robots directly. Fortune did not say how many people were affected.

It is the second time OpenAI has broken up a robotics group: it wound down its first team in 2020 and rebuilt one in 2024. Fortune found OpenAI's booth at this week's CoRL robotics conference in Austin empty.

Analysis — why it matters. Analysis: the move mirrors a wider industry bet that one large general model can serve as the brain for robots, rather than specialised robotics models.

Caveat. The account rests on Fortune's reporting and OpenAI's confirmation to it; OpenAI has published no statement of its own.

UPDATE: Nvidia's board approached Arm chief Rene Haas about succeeding Jensen Huang, the FT reports; the board calls talk of candidates premature

Published: 13 Nov 2026

Update to yesterday's editions on the Nvidia scandal and Jensen Huang's resignation. The new development: Nvidia's board has approached Arm Holdings chief executive Rene Haas in its search for a successor, the Financial Times reported on Friday, citing people familiar with the matter. Reuters, which could not verify the report, says he is one of several candidates and may not be offered the job or accept it.

The board said it was still selecting a search firm and had not formally engaged any candidates. "Any speculation regarding individual candidates, whether internal or external, is premature," it said in a statement quoted by Reuters. Chief financial officer Colette Kress remains interim chief executive.

Haas spent seven years at Nvidia as vice-president and general manager of its computing products business before joining Arm in 2013, and has led Arm since 2022. Arm declined to comment. Reuters also relayed a Wired report that an internal post urging the board to weigh staff sentiment has drawn more than 1,600 comments, several calling for Huang's return; Nvidia declined to comment on it.

Analysis — why it matters. Analysis: the succession now sits alongside the national-security inquiry. Nvidia reports quarterly results on 17 November under an interim chief, and who is chosen will shape its dealings with Washington.

Caveat. The approach to Haas rests on anonymous sources cited by the FT; the FT article is paywalled and was not read, so this account comes from Reuters' summary as published by The Straits Times.

Industry & policy New

Federal Reserve survey: 87% of market contacts now name AI as a salient risk to the US financial system

Published: 13 Nov 2026

The Federal Reserve's twice-yearly Financial Stability Report, published on Friday, says AI was the top-cited risk in its survey of market contacts. In September and October the New York Fed heard from 23 contacts at broker-dealers, banks, investment funds and advisory firms; 87% cited AI, up from 50% in the spring survey. A correction in risk assets came next, at 52%.

Respondents named three worries: high equity valuations that could fall if earnings disappoint, cyberattacks either by state actors or caused by frontier AI models themselves, and complex forms of AI-related financing.

A separate box on the data-centre build-out says debt issued to finance advanced computing rose to more than $680 billion by October 2026, about five times the $130 billion of 2023, and now makes up about 31% of non-financial investment-grade corporate issuance.

Analysis — why it matters. Analysis: this is the central bank putting numbers on the AI-debt worry that has been moving markets, and it lands days before Nvidia's results and Anthropic's listing.

Caveat. The survey reflects the views of 23 contacts, not the Fed's own assessment, as the report itself says; it is not a forecast.

Industry & policy New

US grounds all departures after an air traffic communications failure; officials look at the FAA's new AI planning tool, whose maker denies any link

Published: 13 Nov 2026

The FAA ordered a nationwide ground stop for all US airports on Friday afternoon after communication problems hit multiple air route traffic control centres, its statement said. The FAA Command Center cancelled the stop at 01:19 UTC on Saturday, 20:19 Eastern time on Friday, its advisory shows. It was the second nationwide ground stop since the 11 September attacks, GeekWire notes.

The timing drew attention to SMART, an AI-supported tool from Air Space Intelligence that recommends routes and schedules from about 200 data streams. The FAA said on Tuesday it was expanding it to the airspace of all 22 en route centres. Asked about a link, Homeland Security Secretary Markwayne Mullin said "We're looking at that" and that nothing could be ruled out, while describing the problem as a telecommunications outage of unknown cause.

Air Space Intelligence said the ground stop was not caused by SMART or its integration with the FAA, AL.com and other outlets report. SMART does not control aircraft: FAA specialists review its recommendations and local facilities decide whether to use them. Mullin also said he saw no connection to the Microsoft 365 outage that began the day before.

Analysis — why it matters. Analysis: the episode shows how quickly an AI system gets blamed when critical infrastructure fails, and how much rides on the investigation's findings for AI in air traffic management.

Caveat. No cause has been established. There is no evidence that SMART caused the failure; officials say only that they are examining it.

Industry & policy New

A Virginia software firm called Mythos sues Anthropic for trademark infringement over its Mythos models

Published: 13 Nov 2026

Global Printing Inc., which trades as Mythos and sells donor-communication software to universities and nonprofits, sued Anthropic in the US District Court for the Eastern District of Virginia on Friday, case 1:26-cv-03756, the court docket shows.

The complaint says the firm has used the MYTHOS name since 2015 and holds a 2020 federal registration for software-as-a-service. It says the Patent and Trademark Office refused Anthropic's two applications for CLAUDE MYTHOS in non-final office actions on 20 October, citing that registration, and that customers have already been confused.

It seeks damages, Anthropic's profits from Mythos, trebled damages for wilful infringement and an injunction against Anthropic's use of the name. The complaint notes that the plaintiff itself uses Claude in its product.

Analysis — why it matters. Analysis: an injunction would force a rename of Anthropic's top model line less than a week before its stock market debut, though such cases usually take months.

Caveat. These are the plaintiff's allegations; Anthropic had not responded publicly when this edition went to press, and nothing has been decided.
