Models New

Deep Principle releases Hydrogen-1.0, a roughly 753-billion-parameter open-weight model for chemistry, materials and life sciences built on GLM-5.3

Published: 2 Nov 2026

Hangzhou-based Deep Principle released Hydrogen-1.0, a scientific model made by post-training Z.ai's GLM-5.3 on chemistry, materials-science and life-sciences data, together with laboratory records and agent trajectories from its own labs. The weights are on Hugging Face under an MIT licence, which lists about 753 billion parameters.

MarkTechPost reports a mixture-of-experts design with a 1-million-token context and 128,000-token maximum output, plus NVFP4 and MXFP4 builds. Citing the company's figures, it reports 38.3% on FrontierScience-Research against 33.3% for GPT-6 Astra, roughly matching Kimi K3 on GPQA-Diamond, while trailing GPT-6 Astra on NMR structure elucidation.

Deep Principle presents it as an open, inspectable alternative to the proprietary models behind recent AI science results. The full training datasets and recipes are not released, according to MarkTechPost.

Analysis — why it matters. Analysis: an open-weight model aimed at lab work lets researchers inspect and adapt what proprietary science agents keep closed, though its value rests on benchmarks the company ran itself.

Caveat. All benchmark results are self-reported and not independently reproduced. The announcement carries no timestamp: MarkTechPost reported it at 18:41 UTC on Monday, but the model files were last updated at 09:00 UTC, so the release may slightly predate this window. The afternoon edition did not carry it.

Coding & agents New

Anthropic opens message threads in beta, so Claude agents send only new turns and wait about 35% less on very long sessions

Published: 2 Nov 2026

Anthropic released message threads in public beta on the Claude Platform on Monday. An agent sends the full conversation once to start a thread; after that, each Messages API request carries only the new messages and the ID of the previous response.

Anthropic says threads are already on by default for most Claude Code users, and that on requests of 500,000 to 1 million tokens the median time to first token fell by about 35%. Claude still reads the whole conversation on every turn, so token usage does not change; standard token rates and existing rate limits apply.

Threads are held in memory, encrypted and not written to disk, and expire five minutes after the last request, after which the client resends the full conversation. The beta is offered on the native Claude Platform, Claude Platform on AWS, Claude in Microsoft Foundry and Claude models on Google Cloud.

Analysis — why it matters. Analysis: long-running coding agents spend real time re-uploading their own history. This attacks latency, not cost, so bills for long agent sessions stay the same.

Caveat. The 35% figure is Anthropic's own median on Claude Code traffic; no independent test was available at press time. The Next Web reports that threads are not eligible for zero-data-retention or HIPAA setups; that limit was not visible on Anthropic's announcement page.

Coding & agents New

DeepSeek adds a 'creator mode' to its open-source Harness agent, letting users build plugins by describing them in chat

Published: 2 Nov 2026

DeepSeek introduced creator mode for DeepSeek Harness, its open-source agent platform, on Monday, Quartz reported, citing the company's post on X. The Harness page says users can install plugins, or create them through chat in creator mode, to extend the agent's tools, skills and interface.

The company listed a design workspace, a sandbox game and a 3D LLM visualizer as examples. Bloomberg, as cited by Quartz, reported demonstrations of plugins for 3D model generation and a video-editing workbench built from plain-English prompts. DeepSeek says Harness is still in preview, with evolving core plugins and APIs; it can be launched from npm or cloned from its GitHub repository.

It follows the upgraded coding-agent prototype DeepSeek released on Saturday, which this journal covered on 1 November.

Analysis — why it matters. Analysis: DeepSeek is competing on the agent shell as well as on models. Letting users extend the harness through chat lowers the cost of building on it, and widens what a plugin can touch.

Caveat. The examples are the company's own demonstrations; this journal did not test creator mode. DeepSeek describes Harness as a preview whose APIs may change.

Industry & policy New

Google's threat researchers say hijacked corporate AI accounts ran up more than $100,000 of charges a day, with stolen access resold through 'LLM mixers'

Published: 2 Nov 2026

Google Threat Intelligence Group published a report on Monday on LLMJacking, in which attackers take over an organisation's access to large language models. Google's Mandiant responded to multiple such incidents in 2026, across North America, South America and Asia Pacific, in financial services, retail, technology and transportation.

In individual incidents, fraudulent charges exceeded $100,000 in a single day, and cumulative charges at some organisations reached $1 million before access was revoked. The main way in was exposed or stolen cloud identity tokens, including service-account keys, IAM credentials and GitHub personal access tokens; some attackers also probed model endpoints left open to the internet.

The report describes a division of labour: some actors gain access while others run LLM mixers, gateways that pool stolen credentials and resell model access. In one case, more than 500,000 API calls in under eight hours powered a routing service called Jet Night, which passed nearly $1 million in token costs to the victim and advertised an unlimited plan for about $60 a month.

Analysis — why it matters. Analysis: model API keys are now billable credentials worth stealing for their own sake. Spending alerts and key hygiene become security controls, not just finance tools.

Caveat. The figures come from Google's own incident-response cases and are not a market-wide estimate. Google's view that Jet Night relies on stolen keys is an inference from its below-market pricing.

Industry & policy New

SUSE to cut about 10% of its workforce, with its chief executive citing automation and AI

Published: 2 Nov 2026, 16:30 CET

SUSE, the open-source enterprise software company owned by private-equity firm EQT, is cutting about 10% of its global workforce, The Register reported, citing an email sent on Monday by chief executive Dirk-Peter van Leeuwen to its roughly 1,900 staff.

In the memo, van Leeuwen wrote that 'automation and AI are fundamentally shifting how software companies operate' and that SUSE's current organisational design and cost base would not carry it forward. Affected staff are to be told by 4 November, and the rest of the company will hear its fiscal 2027 strategy on 16 November. The roles at risk were not specified.

The Register notes that SUSE asked long-serving staff to consider voluntary separation in September.

Analysis — why it matters. Analysis: another software vendor is naming AI as a reason for cuts. The memo gives no figures showing how much work AI tooling actually replaced.

Caveat. The memo was reported by The Register; SUSE had not published it. The claim that AI drove the cuts is the company's own and cannot be checked independently.

Industry & policy New

42AI comes out of stealth with $62.5 million and a 'behavioral world model' it says predicts how individual traders will act

Published: 2 Nov 2026, 17:30 CET

42AI emerged from stealth with $62.5 million in seed funding and a transformer-based 'behavioral world model' that, it says, estimates the probability of a person's decisions in unfamiliar situations, TechCrunch reported. The company says it works with 10 large brokerages, which use it to manage liquidity, margin requirements and hedging exposure.

Chief technology officer Ehud Ezra said the model draws on signals such as onboarding, support calls, money transfers and trades, including sentiment and voice in customer conversations. Chief executive Amit Gal-Or said revenue is above 'seven figures'.

The seed money came over three years from the founders and Infinity Group, which was founded by Gal-Or's father, Amir Gal-Or, 42AI's chairman; it was not all raised from outside investors.

Analysis — why it matters. Analysis: predicting behaviour from customer calls and voice raises consent and privacy questions for regulated brokers, whatever the accuracy.

Caveat. Performance and revenue claims are the company's own, as told to TechCrunch; no independent evaluation was published.
