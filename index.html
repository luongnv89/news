Models New

Meta upgrades the models behind Muse and Meta AI with skills from Muse Wonder, and adds Spaces to Muse

Published: 6 Nov 2026

Meta is rolling out specialized versions of its existing models, trained using its Muse Wonder series, to people who use Muse and Meta AI. They bring two Muse Wonder capabilities: Active Memory, which judges which earlier information still matters and which is stale, and Active Focus, which keeps working toward a goal when something goes wrong.

Meta says the improved Muse models show a 2.4x reduction in forgetting information critical to a user's goal, and a 67% improvement in completing everyday tasks where unexpected issues arise. On its own Active Memory evaluation the current Muse model uses critical context correctly in 90% of tasks, against 76% for the previous one, and on Meta's Personal Superintelligence Index it scores 47% against 38%.

Separately, Meta launched Spaces in Muse: a user describes an idea in chat and Muse builds a tracker, planner or project board in the sidebar, which can stay up to date from connected apps, TechCrunch reports.

Caveat. All figures are Meta's own evaluations, with no independent results; Meta has not named the models or given their sizes.

Coding & agents New

Visa's shopping benchmark: AI agents failed 42.8% of runs, and most failed runs still ended in a purchase

Published: 5 Nov 2026

Visa released the Visa Agentic Commerce Benchmark on Thursday 5 November; this paper did not carry it then. In the first round, 22 AI models from 12 labs took on 15 shopping tasks such as buying a TV, a laptop or a personal-care appliance. To pass, a model had to uncover the shopper's unstated requirements through conversation and buy only if something fitted, or decline.

Visa's release, as reported by PYMNTS, says 42.8% of test runs failed. The best models passed 82.2% of their runs and the worst 28.9%. In 54% of failed runs the agent still made a purchase, either one that missed the customer's requirements or one made before it had found them all. The five most expensive models per run passed 67.1% of runs, the five cheapest 50.2%.

Visa says models that passed a scenario often failed when made to repeat it. It plans to add scenarios and extend the tests to checkout, payment and order placement, and the benchmark is open for anyone to try on its Visa Payments Frontier site.

Analysis — why it matters. Analysis: the failure that matters most here is not an agent giving up but one confidently buying the wrong thing, which is exactly where payment networks and merchants carry the risk.

Caveat. Visa designed and ran the benchmark, which covers only three product categories; its release does not say which model scored what.

Research New

Physics start-up PSI says a non-public Anthropic model found a machine-checked proof of the Aaronson-Ambainis conjecture

Published: 6 Nov 2026

Physical Superintelligence (PSI) said on Friday that a research system it built, running a non-public Anthropic model, found a proof of the Aaronson-Ambainis conjecture, open since 2008. The conjecture concerns when quantum computers can be exponentially faster than ordinary ones: if it is true, they are at most polynomially faster on random inputs that lack special structure.

PSI says the system had a complete proof during the third day of the run and that Lean, a proof assistant that checks every step by computer, accepted it. The system then improved the result twice, and Lean accepted the final version. PSI has published a paper and the Lean proofs and code on GitHub.

Guy Blanc, Jordan Docter, Carmen Strassle and Li-Yang Tan of Stanford, who settled a special case in August, are reading the proof and writing a shorter exposition for experts, PSI says; it quotes them calling it a huge result. Business Insider reports it is not clear which unreleased model PSI used, and Anthropic did not respond to its request for comment.

Analysis — why it matters. Analysis: if it holds, this is a result on a central question in quantum complexity found largely by an AI system, and the Lean code lets anyone re-run the checks.

Caveat. This is PSI's own claim and has not been peer reviewed. The Anthropic model is not named and cannot be tested by outsiders. A Lean check proves the formal statements; experts still need to confirm that they match the conjecture as stated.

Research New

OpenAI's BioBasicsBench tests agents on raw wet-lab data; GPT-6 Astra earns 66.7% of the credit

Published: 6 Nov 2026

OpenAI published BioBasicsBench, a benchmark built with the enzyme-design company Ligo that asks AI agents to read raw evidence from routine wet-lab work: gel and blot images, plate photos, microscopy, sequencing reads, instrument exports and bench videos. Its 100 tasks cover protein methods, imaging and cytometry, assays, nucleic acid methods and molecular cloning, and test whether an agent can spot errors and say when the evidence cannot support a conclusion.

Ligo scientists wrote the tasks from their own experiments with grading rubrics, and each task had an internal review, an external review by a PhD-level wet-lab scientist and a final check. OpenAI ran seven of its models in 37 settings, five attempts per task, for 18,500 results.

At the xhigh reasoning setting, GPT-6 Astra earned 66.7% of the available credit, GPT-6.1 Sol 62.0% and GPT-5.6 Sol 43.8%, with Astra using about half Sol's output tokens per task. In one example, Astra noticed that a control plate was labelled for the wrong antibiotic, which GPT-5.6 Sol missed.

Analysis — why it matters. Analysis: the benchmark targets the routine, messy analysis that real lab work depends on, and OpenAI says it also uses the results to track what model gains mean for biosafety and biosecurity.

Caveat. OpenAI built the benchmark with a partner and tested only its own models; the scores are its own measurements.

Industry & policy New

OpenAI closes the first $11 billion of a round at a $1.25 trillion valuation, Bloomberg reports

Published: 6 Nov 2026

OpenAI has closed $11 billion of new funding, the first part of a round that values it at $1.25 trillion, Bloomberg News reported on Friday, citing people familiar with the matter. The money had been wired as of Friday, and the rest of the round, which could reach $20 billion in total, is expected in the coming weeks, one person said. OpenAI declined to comment.

According to Bloomberg, OpenAI first set out to raise $30 billion or more at a $1.4 trillion valuation, then scaled back its plans during this week's sell-off in AI shares. It raised $122 billion in March at an $852 billion valuation.

TechCrunch notes the report came hours after Bloomberg said some prospective buyers of shares in Anthropic's IPO believe its valuation could be as low as $1.1 trillion.

Analysis — why it matters. Analysis: the round is still large, but it is smaller and priced lower than OpenAI first sought, a measure of how far sentiment toward AI spending has turned since the midterm elections.

Caveat. The figures come from anonymous sources; OpenAI has not confirmed the amount or the valuation, and the round is not complete.

Industry & policy New

Microsoft is winding down its rental of AI computing from Oracle, Bloomberg reports

Published: 6 Nov 2026

Microsoft is winding down an arrangement to rent AI computing from Oracle, part of a larger reassessment of billions of dollars in cloud partnerships, Bloomberg News reported on Friday, citing people familiar with the matter. Microsoft has been turning off the rented capacity in recent months after declining to renew the contract, the report said.

The capacity became less useful because it no longer fit Microsoft's specifications; the Oracle hardware used older-generation Nvidia chips, according to the people. An Oracle spokesperson told Reuters that demand for its AI infrastructure remains strong, including for earlier-generation GPUs. Microsoft did not immediately respond to Reuters.

Oracle announced the multi-year deal in 2023, saying Microsoft would use its cloud's AI infrastructure for the AI version of Bing; terms were not disclosed. Reuters links the move to other signs that Microsoft is rethinking how it buys capacity, including the IREN delay this paper reported on Friday morning.

Analysis — why it matters. Analysis: the stated reason is the age of the chips rather than falling demand, but it is another case of a hyperscaler cutting rented capacity in the same week investors began to doubt AI spending.

Caveat. Bloomberg's report relies on anonymous sources, and Microsoft has not commented.

Industry & policy New

Google takes over Crusoe's unfinished Wyoming data-centre site after pledging $17 billion for it

Published: 6 Nov 2026

Crusoe confirmed on Friday that it has sold its unfinished data-centre project in southeast Wyoming to Google, a day after Google announced a $17 billion investment to build its first Wyoming data centre there. Financial terms were not disclosed, Reuters reports.

Crusoe says it completed the pre-development work, including site selection, government approvals and the power strategy. Google is building the campus with Tallgrass Energy, Black Hills Energy, American Electric Power and Related Digital. TechCrunch reports that Google had raised concerns about the project's cost and timeline under Crusoe; Bloomberg first reported the handover in June.

Crusoe is still developing another campus for Google near Amarillo, Texas. Its chief executive, Chase Lochmiller, said the company has more than 6 gigawatts of contracted capacity, according to Reuters.

Analysis — why it matters. Analysis: a hyperscaler taking a troubled site in-house shows how hard it is for independent developers to deliver giant campuses on time, at a moment when voters have turned against data centres.
