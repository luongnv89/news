Coding & agents New

Signal blocks screenshots on iOS 27 and Android to stop operating-system AI agents from reading chats

Published: 20 Nov 2026

Signal president Meredith Whittaker wrote on Friday that the app will now restrict agentic systems built into iOS 27 and, on Android, block screen capture when it detects an AI assistant trying to read the Signal screen. She names Google's Gemini Intelligence and Apple's Onscreen Awareness for Siri AI, which read on-screen content to answer users' requests.

Signal calls these integrations an existential threat to its mission, saying data captured from the screen ends up in whatever local database or remote server the agent uses, outside Signal's control. Last year it used the same screenshot block against Microsoft's Recall on Windows.

Users can turn screenshots back on in settings, but Whittaker warns this could expose their and their contacts' messages to the agents, The Verge reports. She said Google, Apple and Microsoft give developers no easy way to restrict such access, which is why Signal cannot yet do the same on macOS.

Analysis — why it matters. Editorial analysis: as assistants gain the right to read and act across apps, an encrypted messenger is now blocking the operating system's own AI, and asking the platform owners for proper controls.

Caveat. Detection on Android depends on what Signal can see of an assistant's behaviour; Signal does not list which Android versions or assistants are covered.

Research New

Anthropic mathematician Levent Alpöge says he and Claude have proved the Riemann hypothesis; the claim is not yet checked by others

Published: 20 Nov 2026

Levent Alpöge, a mathematician who works at Anthropic, posted a statement on his website on Friday saying: 'I believe I have, working with Claude, discovered a proof of the Riemann Hypothesis.' He writes that the method extends to the Generalized Riemann Hypothesis and that he and Claude have formalized the arguments in the Lean proof assistant, with the files posted in 14 parts alongside the statement.

The Riemann hypothesis, proposed in 1859, concerns where the zeros of the Riemann zeta function lie and governs how prime numbers are distributed; it is one of the Clay Institute's million-dollar Millennium Prize Problems. Scientific American, which reported the claim on Friday, wrote that experts should not need long to check whether the statement is coded correctly in Lean and whether the proof holds.

Alpöge describes the argument as incredibly long at the moment and says he intends to understand it, simplify it and write it up properly. No paper or outside review accompanies the statement.

Background (August 2026): Anthropic reported then that an unreleased research version of Claude had raised the proven share of zeta zeros on the critical line from 41.6% to 67.2%, a related but much smaller result; that post said Anthropic did not expect those techniques to lead to a proof of the hypothesis itself.

Analysis — why it matters. Editorial analysis: if the proof survives checking, it would be the first AI-assisted solution of a Millennium Prize Problem; until then it is one researcher's claim, made by an employee of the company whose model he used.

Caveat. Unverified claim: no independent mathematician or published check of the Lean files had confirmed it when this edition closed. A formal proof is only as good as the statement it encodes.

Industry & policy Updated

UPDATE: Anthropic closes its offering at $93.5 billion after underwriters buy 147 million extra shares, as the stock slips before Friday's open

Published: 20 Nov 2026

Update to this morning's edition, which reported Anthropic's first day on Nasdaq and a combined $107.3 billion from the offering and a private placement. The new development: Anthropic announced on Friday that the offering has closed, with its underwriters exercising in full their option to buy 147 million more shares at $83.

That brings the offering to 1.127 billion shares and about $93.5 billion in gross proceeds, of which about $91.6 billion goes to Anthropic; selling stockholders received the rest. The concurrent $26 billion private placement of Class N shares to AMD, Broadcom, Google, Micron, Microsoft, Nvidia and TSMC Partners also closed, for about $119.5 billion in total before fees, the company says.

The shares lost ground before Friday's session: CNBC reported them 6.38% lower in premarket trading at 4.02 a.m. New York time, after Thursday's close of $97.17, with Nvidia and AMD also down.

Analysis — why it matters. Editorial analysis: the extra shares add $12 billion of cash on top of an already record raise, while the first full day of trading shows how quickly enthusiasm for the listing is being tested.

Caveat. The premarket figure is a snapshot from early Friday; this edition closed before a full Friday session and does not report an intraday price.

Industry & policy New

Cursor tells House committees it may phase out Chinese open-weight models over 'geopolitical risk', CNBC reports

Published: 20 Nov 2026, 15:04 CET

Cursor, the AI coding tool SpaceX bought in August, plans to 'explore phasing out PRC-developed open-weight models because of geopolitical risk', according to a report by the House Committee on Homeland Security and the House Select Committee on China that CNBC saw ahead of its publication on Friday. Cursor built its Composer 2 coding model on Moonshot AI's Kimi K2.5.

The report follows a months-long investigation into US companies' use of Chinese models; Airbnb and DoorDash also engaged with it. It finds that cost, customisation and near-frontier performance drive adoption of Chinese models, and that the US lacks an open-weight alternative at the 'price and performance the market demands'.

Its recommendations include a national American open-weight initiative combining public computing power, shared evaluation tools and lawful access to US frontier models for training, and extending October's ban on DeepSeek in federal civilian systems to all PRC-developed models in federal systems and federal contracts.

Analysis — why it matters. Editorial analysis: a well-known US product built on a Chinese base model is backing away under congressional pressure, while the committees concede the cost case for those models.

Caveat. Reported by CNBC from a report not yet public when this edition closed; Cursor, SpaceX and DoorDash did not comment, and 'explore phasing out' is not a decision.

Industry & policy New

SpaceX's lenders sought to stop it launching the Nvidia chips that secure its new debt into orbit, the FT reports

Published: 20 Nov 2026, 15:15 CET

Lenders behind SpaceX's $43 billion chip-backed debt package tried to bar the company from sending the Nvidia chips that serve as collateral into space for Elon Musk's planned orbital data centres, according to a Financial Times report republished by Ars Technica. They feared they could not reclaim and sell the chips after a default; SpaceX has said it plans to use these chips on Earth.

It is not certain the restriction made the final documents, the report says. SpaceX sold more than $30 billion of the debt to large investors on Thursday in a financing called Project Saturn; the largest part is a $25 billion investment-grade bond backed by SpaceX's five-year lease payments for the chips, issued through a special-purpose vehicle that keeps the debt off its balance sheet.

Analysis — why it matters. Editorial analysis: chip-backed borrowing is becoming a standard way to fund AI build-outs, and lenders are already writing the physical location of the collateral into the terms.

Caveat. Based on people familiar with the matter; Bloomberg's account of the bond sale and the FT original are paywalled and were not read.

Industry & policy New

Former Qwen chief Lin Junyang's Pragmatik Labs raises money at a $5 billion valuation, Bloomberg reports

Published: 20 Nov 2026

Pragmatik Labs, the Shanghai AI start-up Lin Junyang set up over the summer after leaving Alibaba, where he oversaw the Qwen family of models, has closed a round valuing it at $5 billion, Bloomberg reported, according to Quartz. In August its first financing, of several hundred million dollars, valued it at $2 billion.

The new round raised tens of millions of dollars from investors including HyT Capital, CDH Investments and carmaker Geely. Pragmatik builds AI agents for virtual and real-world settings. None of the companies responded to Bloomberg's requests for comment.

Analysis — why it matters. Editorial analysis: investors are pricing China's new research-led labs at multiples of their August value, even as US chip curbs limit the compute they can buy.

Caveat. Based on unnamed people familiar with the matter; Bloomberg's article is paywalled and was read through Quartz's summary.
