### Google launches Gemini Decision 1, a model that scores up to 1,000 yes/no or multiple-choice questions in one pass

*Models · New*

**Published:** 11 Nov 2026, 19:00 CET

Google introduced the **Gemini Decisions API** on 11 November, powered by a new model, **Gemini Decision 1** (gemini-decision-1). Instead of generating text, it scores a fixed set of options and returns probabilities and confidence scores, for tasks such as tool selection, policy checks, model routing and grading context.

One request carries a single input of text, images, audio, video or PDFs, up to 256K tokens, plus **up to 1,000 independent questions**: yes/no checks, choices among 2 to 255 options, or scores on a rubric. Google says it answers in 150 to 250 milliseconds end to end and charges **$0.10 per million input tokens with no output charge**; the shared input is billed once per request. It is available in Google AI Studio with a no-cost tier.

Google claims the model is nearly five times cheaper on its benchmark suite than Gemini 3.5 Flash-Lite. It enters a field where TypeSafe's Jev and OpenAI's Decisions API already sell similar decision models, VentureBeat notes.

**Analysis — why it matters:** *Editorial analysis: agent builders make many small judgments per task; **a cheap, fast scorer for those choices** could cut costs more than another chat model would.*

**Caveat:** The speed and cost comparisons are Google's own claims on its own benchmark suite. The Decisions API does not support context caching or the Batch API.

### United Airlines bars unauthorised third-party AI agents from booking, changing or checking in for its flights

*Coding & agents · New*

**Published:** 11 Nov 2026

United Airlines said on Wednesday 11 November that it is **restricting access by unauthorised third-party AI agents** and assistants, both on its own website and app and indirectly through travel agencies and aggregators. Its help page says any agent, bot, browser extension or browser feature that searches, books, buys, manages or collects information on united.com or in the United app without the airline's permission is unauthorised.

United says sharing account credentials with such assistants can expose personal data and lead to unintended purchases, account changes or use of MileagePlus miles, and that automated check-in could breach rules requiring a human to confirm a bag carries no banned items. Bookings an agent made **before 11 November will not be cancelled**, travellers can still use AI to plan trips without touching United's systems, and assistive technology for customers with disabilities is not affected. Travel agencies may use AI tools only with United's permission.

United says it does not yet work with any third-party AI agents. CNBC notes that companies are split: Amazon has blocked Meta's Muse agent while Walmart has partnered with it.

**Analysis — why it matters:** *Editorial analysis: this is one of the first large consumer brands to write an **agent ban into its booking rules**, a sign that agents may need permission deals, not just credentials, to transact.*

**Caveat:** United has not said how it will detect agents in practice. An analyst quoted by CBS News suggests United also wants to keep control of how its fares are sold; United itself cites security and regulation.

### SpaceXSI takes Grok Bot out of beta with shared team bots that join Slack, Microsoft Teams and video calls

*Coding & agents · New*

**Published:** 11 Nov 2026

SpaceXSI launched **Grok Bot 1.0** on 11 November, ending the beta of its always-on AI agents. Team Bots can be shared with people or groups and brought into **Slack, Microsoft Teams and video calls**, where they follow multi-person discussions and take part through the chat.

The company says the bots now **offer to take on work unprompted**: they monitor projects, follow up on unfinished tasks and bring people in when a decision needs human judgment. It also says they are faster and more reliable at browser work, documents and slide decks, and adds team settings and usage analytics. Team Bots are on Team and Enterprise plans; meetings are on Team, Enterprise and Ultra plans and must be switched on by Enterprise admins.

**Analysis — why it matters:** *Editorial analysis: agents are moving from personal assistants to shared colleagues inside work chat, which raises new questions about whose permissions a shared bot uses.*

**Caveat:** The capability claims come from SpaceXSI's launch post and have not been independently tested.

### UPDATE: Reviewers publish the Lean code behind OpenAI's Yang-Mills existence proof and say it holds, but not for the mass gap

*Research · Updated*

**Published:** 11 Nov 2026, 04:37 CET

Update to the 27 October edition, which reported that OpenAI's maths advisers asked it to release its claimed Yang-Mills proof. The new development: on the night of 10 November, US time, the review committee led by physicist David Gross **published its assessment and the checked Lean code** on GitHub, as its 9 November statement had promised; yesterday morning's edition said the code could not yet be found.

The assessment, dated 10 November, concerns OpenAI's submission of 28 October. In the group's opinion it proves that a relativistic quantum field theory satisfying the Wightman axioms **can be constructed as a scaling limit of four-dimensional lattice gauge theory** for any simple compact gauge group. It **does not prove a mass gap** or that the infinite-volume limit is unique, so the Millennium Prize problem is not solved. A second OpenAI submission, of 4 November, is listed as work in progress.

Scientific American reports that the reviewers wrote they used OpenAI's Astra model to help digest the Lean code and "do not claim to fully understand the proof yet". The package includes reproduction instructions so others can rerun the formal checks.

**Analysis — why it matters:** *Editorial analysis: with the code public, the claim can now be checked by anyone, not only by a committee the company chose.*

**Caveat:** The committee was appointed by OpenAI, and its own repository says posting a submission is **not an endorsement or certification**. Outside mathematicians have only just received the material.

*Industry & policy · Updated*

**Published:** 11 Nov 2026

The meeting followed a New York Times report, citing five people who worked at Nvidia, that Huang also had a relationship with **Kimberly Powell, Nvidia's vice president of healthcare**, who reports to him. ### Meta's longtime infrastructure chief Santosh Janardhan will step down, handing over to former AWS executive Dave Brown

*Industry & policy · New*

**Published:** 11 Nov 2026

**Santosh Janardhan**, Meta's head of infrastructure and co-head of engineering, told staff on 11 November that he will step down, according to an internal memo first reported by Bloomberg and also obtained by CNBC. He has been at the company since 2009 and wrote that it is "time to pass the baton".

**Dave Brown**, who joined Meta this year after about two decades at Amazon Web Services, will succeed him. Janardhan will support the transition through the **first quarter of 2027**, and a Meta representative told TechCrunch he is moving into an advisory role on Meta Compute, the initiative that plans Meta's computing capacity for AI. The memo gave no reason for the change.

**Analysis — why it matters:** *Editorial analysis: Meta expects **$135 billion to $145 billion** of capital spending this year, mostly for AI data centres, so the person running that build-out matters to its AI plans.*
