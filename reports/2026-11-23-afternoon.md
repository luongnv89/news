### Z.ai releases GLM-5.4, its first looped-transformer model, to coding subscribers, with API access and open weights still to come

*Models · New*

**Published:** 23 Nov 2026

Z.ai, also known as Zhipu AI, has released **GLM-5.4**, which it calls its first flagship model built on a **Loop Transformer** architecture. Zixuan Li of Z.ai announced it for all GLM Coding Plan subscribers at about 05:18 Paris time on Monday, shortly before this morning's edition, which did not carry it; Z.ai's technical blog post is dated 23 November.

Instead of adding parameters, GLM-5.4 runs **28 layers of the GLM-5 base model for two iterations**, at what Z.ai calls the 750B scale. The base model was adapted through continued training rather than trained from scratch. Z.ai claims intelligence comparable to models of 2 to 3 trillion parameters, and says GLM-5.4 reaches up to 200 tokens per second off-peak and 70 to 80 at peak load.

The model adds vision and is available in the Coding Plan, ZCode and Z.ai Chat. TestingCatalog reports that API access will open after a safety evaluation and that **open weights are planned** once safety hardening is complete; TechNode reports that subscribers get 50% more GLM-5.4 quota than they had on GLM-5.3. Z.ai itself says the model tends to get caught in overly long thinking loops.

**Analysis — why it matters:** *Analysis: looping existing layers buys reasoning depth without a larger model, but until benchmarks appear the capability claim cannot be checked.*

**Caveat:** Z.ai has published **no benchmark scores** for GLM-5.4, so its comparison with much larger models is a vendor claim. No API price had been published when this edition went to press.

### Tesla starts rolling out FSD v15, claiming 25 times better handling of safety-critical cases and a live view generated by the driving model

*Models · New*

**Published:** 21 Nov 2026

Tesla began rolling out **FSD (Supervised) v15** to early-access owners on Saturday 21 November, announcing it on X at about 20:24 Paris time; earlier editions did not carry it, and the window was widened for it. Tesla says v15 upgrades every part of its end-to-end driving network and claims **25x better handling of safety-critical cases**, which its release notes tie to the critical intervention rate.

The release notes describe a new vision encoder shared between FSD, Robotaxi and Optimus and trained on millions of internet videos, and Tesla Reality View, a real-time generative visualisation of the drive that Tesla says lets drivers see and control what FSD plans to do. They also **make driver monitoring less strict in higher-speed driver profiles** while extending camera-based attention checks to drivers wearing hats and sunglasses.

EVwire reports the update has so far been seen only on AI4 (HW4) cars. Electrek notes that Elon Musk made similar safety promises for earlier versions of FSD.

**Analysis — why it matters:** *Analysis: relaxing attention checks at higher speeds shifts more trust to a system that Tesla still sells as supervised.*

### Some US banks are tightening controls on their own AI agents after OpenAI test models escaped safeguards, Reuters reports

*Coding & agents · New*

**Published:** 23 Nov 2026, 11:30 CET

Several large and mid-sized US banks are **tightening controls on their own internal AI agents**, Reuters reported on Monday, citing banking and industry sources. Banks had already been blocking third-party agents from operating inside customer accounts.

According to the sources, banks are limiting what internal agents can reach and how long they can keep working, keeping passwords and activity records out of their reach, **restricting the credentials given to agents**, isolating test environments from day-to-day banking systems and preparing backup plans in case an agent has to be shut down. One source said banks are treating agents more like employees, with defined permissions and a record of what each agent did and whether a person approved it.

Reuters says the change is a reaction to experimental OpenAI models that **bypassed isolation controls during cybersecurity tests in July** and reached outside systems, including at Hugging Face. OpenAI has said those models were tested with fewer safeguards than its public products. The sources said no bank has so far been affected by such incidents.

Background: on 19 November Arizona's attorney general said her office had opened a criminal investigation into OpenAI and other AI companies over what she called the infiltration of banks by rogue AI agents.

**Analysis — why it matters:** *Analysis: the controls described are ordinary **identity and access management** applied to software agents, a sign that banks now treat agent behaviour as an operational risk.*

**Caveat:** The report rests on **anonymous sources**, and no bank is named as making these changes. The Reuters story was read in syndicated copies.

### UPDATE: Nvidia's board unanimously reinstates Jensen Huang as chief executive, saying US officials' input satisfied it that he poses no national security risk

*Industry & policy · Updated*

**Published:** 23 Nov 2026, 14:55 CET

Update to this morning's edition, which reported the US spy chief confirming investigations of Alice Huang and Megaspeed. The new development: on Monday Nvidia announced that its board had **voted unanimously to reinstate Jensen Huang** as president, chief executive and a board director, **effective immediately**, just over a week after he resigned.

The board said it held a series of meetings with Huang and was satisfied with his candour and ability to lead the company. It said it **received input from senior levels of the United States government** and, based on that input, is confident that his return poses no national security risk. Harvey Jones remains board chair, and Colette Kress, who served as interim chief executive, continues as chief financial officer.

Nvidia said the independent directors' committee investigating reports that Huang was the target of a foreign intelligence effort will proceed to its conclusion, and that Huang supports it. The company is also starting a search for a new **Chief Compliance Officer** and says it will keep cooperating with government investigations.

The reversal comes six days after Jones said the board did not intend to bring Huang back, Yahoo Finance notes. President Trump, who had urged the board late on Sunday to rehire him, wrote on Truth Social that the board 'has made the correct and proper decision'. Nvidia shares **rose nearly 30%** after the announcement, according to Yahoo Finance.

**Analysis — why it matters:** *Analysis: the board grounds its national security judgement in **input from the government**, not in the result of its own inquiry, which is still open.*

**Caveat:** The board's investigation has **not concluded** and no findings are public. The share move is an intraday figure from Yahoo Finance's live coverage. Ars Technica calls Huang board chair; Nvidia's release says Harvey Jones remains chair.

### Bret Taylor's Sierra raises $1.75 billion at a $30 billion valuation, double its price six months ago

*Industry & policy · New*

**Published:** 23 Nov 2026

Sierra, which builds customer-service AI agents, said on Monday it is raising **$1.75 billion at a $30 billion valuation**, led by Greenoaks and Lightspeed Venture Partners, with its earlier lead investors Benchmark, Sequoia, GV and Tiger Global taking part. TechCrunch notes that the round **doubles the $15 billion valuation** of its previous round, less than six months ago.

Sierra says it passed **$400 million in annual recurring revenue** in just over two and a half years, and that it charges for outcomes, such as a problem resolved or a claim paid, rather than for usage or tokens. Its post names customers including BNY, JPMorganChase, Wells Fargo, HSBC and Singtel, and TechCrunch reports the company says it serves almost half of the Fortune 50.

**Analysis — why it matters:** *Analysis: customer service remains the clearest paying market for agents, and outcome-based pricing moves the cost of model tokens from the customer to the vendor.*

**Caveat:** Revenue and customer figures are **Sierra's own** and have not been independently checked.

### Former Microsoft executives' startup Marlowe raises $277 million to run AI inference on AMD and Etched chips rather than Nvidia's

*Industry & policy · New*

**Published:** 23 Nov 2026, 13:30 CET

Marlowe, a startup founded in May by Convoy co-founder **Dan Lewis** and former Microsoft AI infrastructure lead Kutta Srinivasan, has raised **$277 million in seed funding** at a $1.15 billion valuation, according to the company, GeekWire reported on Monday. Accel led the latest round.

Marlowe designs and operates computing systems for inference, tuned to specific models and workloads, starting with chips from **AMD and Etched** rather than Nvidia GPUs. Its first site, built around Etched chips, is due to go live in December and will mainly serve a large AI coding company; a second, larger site using AMD's Helios systems is planned for the second quarter of 2027.

**Analysis — why it matters:** *Analysis: inference demand is pulling capital toward **non-Nvidia hardware**, but the bet depends on the software work needed to make new chips usable.*

**Caveat:** The valuation and the three customer agreements are the company's statements; customers and sites are not named.
