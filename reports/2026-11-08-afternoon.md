## The News

### Anthropic paid about $3.4 billion for 1.7 gigawatts of gas-fired generators for its data centres, the Financial Times reports

*Industry & policy · New*

**Published:** 8 Nov 2026

Anthropic has struck a deal worth about **$3.4 billion** for **1.7 gigawatts** of heavy-duty gas-fired generators for its data centres, the Financial Times reported on Sunday, 8 November, citing a person familiar with the matter. According to the report, as carried by Reuters, Anthropic bought the generator commitments in late September from **OnePWR Solutions**, an equipment supplier based in San Antonio, Texas.

Anthropic did not buy OnePWR itself and none of the supplier's employees moved to Anthropic, but the deal included the engineering and construction capabilities needed to install the generators, the report says. Investing.com's summary of the FT report adds that OnePWR had ordered the generators from **Caterpillar** about two years ago but needed financing to complete the purchase, and that Anthropic first planned to become a OnePWR customer before taking over the orders. The FT describes the deal as the largest known single purchase of power-generation equipment by an AI lab.

Reuters said it could not immediately verify the report, and Anthropic and OnePWR did not respond to its requests for comment. The reports do not say where the generators will be installed.

**Analysis — why it matters:** *Editorial analysis: holding generator orders directly, rather than buying power from a supplier, takes an AI lab a step further into the energy business, and it comes weeks before Anthropic's planned stock-market listing. Where the generators end up will matter as opposition to data centres grows in the US and Europe.*

**Caveat:** **Single-source report**: the Financial Times article cites one person familiar with the matter, and it was not read here (paywall); it is known through Reuters' and Investing.com's summaries. Neither Anthropic nor OnePWR has confirmed the deal.

### Staff at the US government's AI safety testing centre have been asked to report informally to the White House, the AP reports

*Industry & policy · New*

**Published:** 8 Nov 2026

Officials at the **Center for Advancing Innovation and Standards for Super Intelligence** (CAISSI), the Commerce Department division that assesses the safety of new AI models, were recently asked to **report informally to an office of the White House**, the Associated Press reported on 8 November, citing two former government officials who spoke on condition of anonymity. The AP does not name the office.

According to the AP, the centre, called the US Center for AI Standards and Innovation until recently, has had **no permanent leader** since a director hired in April was fired within days and his replacement resigned a few months later; the head of its parent institute is acting director. It has stopped publishing its reports on how safely AI systems perform, which Congress began asking about in August, and it is allocated **$10 million a year**, about one-eighth of its UK counterpart's budget.

The White House did not answer the AP's specific questions about CAISSI. In a statement it said President Trump had formally established his Super Intelligence Force and had commissioned a report on AI risks from his Council of Advisors on Science and Technology.

**Analysis — why it matters:** *Editorial analysis: the US government's dedicated AI model tester now has no permanent head, no public reports and, by the AP's account, a line to the White House. Critics quoted by the AP fear its findings could be politicised; others argue the closer tie could bring it more prominence and money.*

**Caveat:** The account rests on **two unnamed former officials**, and the arrangement is described as informal; neither the White House nor the Commerce Department confirmed it.
