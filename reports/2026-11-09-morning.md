### LG AI Research opens its K-EXAONE 2.5 model to all developers through a free API

*Models · New*

**Published:** 9 Nov 2026

LG AI Research said on 9 November that the API for **K-EXAONE 2.5**, its proprietary foundation model, is **available free of charge** through its EXAONE API Platform. Developers, start-ups, companies and research institutions can use it for service development and research by obtaining an API key, the lab said.

LG describes K-EXAONE 2.5 as a technical validation model with additional training on top of the version it released in August. Digital Today reported that the announcement was made at Nvidia AI Day Seoul 2026 and that LG wants to test the model's performance and usability in real services.

LG claims, from internal evaluations on public datasets, scores of **59.2 on SWE-Bench Pro**, 78.0 on BrowseComp, 74.6 on AutomationBench-Openset and 77.3 on Claw-Eval, and says the model is competitive with DeepSeek-V4.1-Flash and GLM-5.3 on agentic tasks. It pitches the model for code generation, debugging, refactoring and test writing.

**Analysis — why it matters:** *Analysis: a free hosted API is a fast way for a national-champion model to win developers away from cheap Chinese open-weight rivals, the comparison LG itself chose. Whether it sticks depends on usage limits and pricing after the free period, which LG has not published.*

**Caveat:** All benchmark figures are **LG's own claims** from internal runs and have not been independently reproduced. LG did not say how long the API will stay free or what rate limits apply, and it did not announce open weights for version 2.5.

### UPDATE: Nvidia's board opens an independent investigation after reports that Jensen Huang was targeted by a foreign intelligence effort, and Huang admits an affair

*Industry & policy · Updated*

**Published:** 8 Nov 2026

Update to yesterday morning's edition, which carried the Financial Times report on a US memo alleging that Jensen Huang had a romantic relationship with Alice Huang, a Megaspeed executive whom US intelligence reportedly believes was recruited by China's Ministry of State Security. The new development: on Sunday 8 November **Nvidia's board announced an independent investigation** in light of recent reporting that Huang has been the target of a foreign intelligence effort. A **committee of independent directors**, assisted by outside independent counsel, will oversee it.

The board said it has no reason to believe that national security or Nvidia secrets were compromised, that its inquiry will examine all available information, and that it has confidence in Huang and his leadership team. The same day Nvidia published an email Huang sent to employees in which he wrote that he **was unfaithful to his wife**, apologised, and said he had fallen short of his responsibility to colleagues, the board and shareholders.

On the national security concerns raised in news reports, Huang wrote that he **supports the investigation and will cooperate fully**. His email does not name Alice Huang. TechCrunch, citing Bloomberg and other outlets, reported that the affair Huang admitted was with her.

Asked by reporters on Sunday whether Huang should stay on, President Donald Trump said he thought so and called him a fine person, adding that he had not spoken to him about it, Bloomberg reported. Bloomberg also said the New York Times reported on Sunday that Secretary of State Marco Rubio had overseen the production of the secret memo on US concerns.

**Analysis — why it matters:** *Analysis: Nvidia sells most of the chips behind the AI build-out and sits at the centre of US export policy toward China, so **a board-level review of its chief executive** matters well beyond one company. The House committees' request for a closed-door briefing on 18 November, carried in yesterday morning's edition, is the next public date to watch.*

**Caveat:** The espionage allegation still rests on a US memo that has not been published and is known through news reports; **no charges have been announced**. Huang's email confirms an affair but does not address the intelligence claims, and the board gave no timetable for its review. The New York Times report on Rubio's role is known here only through Bloomberg's account.

### Trump tells 60 Minutes that AI risks can be managed and that he does not mind guardrails, Reuters reports

*Industry & policy · New*

**Published:** 8 Nov 2026

President Donald Trump said the risks posed by artificial intelligence **can be managed** but signalled openness to safeguards, telling CBS's 60 Minutes that he **"doesn't mind guardrails"**, Reuters reported on Sunday night.

Asked whether the United States should accept the risks of AI to stay ahead of China, Trump said the dangers were manageable and expressed **openness to working with Democrats** on safeguards, according to Reuters. He added that he did not want regulation to leave the United States in "a deep second position" behind China, which he said had no such restrictions.

Reuters noted that Trump has previously favoured letting technology companies police themselves rather than imposing sweeping government restrictions, and that it remains unclear what level of safeguards he would accept.

**Analysis — why it matters:** *Analysis: the remarks come days after Democrats won control of Congress and began preparing AI bills, which this journal covered last week. A president open to **bipartisan AI safeguards** changes the odds for that legislation, but his condition that rules must not put the US behind China leaves the scope wide open.*

**Caveat:** These are interview remarks, not a policy proposal or executive action. Reuters' own page answered HTTP 401 and could not be read by any allowed route, so the report is cited through two syndicated copies; the CBS broadcast itself was not reviewed for this edition.

### Nvidia signs a Level 4 self-driving pact with Kakao Mobility as it pitches physical AI to Korean industry in Seoul

*Industry & policy · New*

**Published:** 9 Nov 2026

Kakao Mobility, the ride-hailing affiliate of Kakao Corp., said on Monday it has signed a **memorandum of understanding with Nvidia** to develop a **Level 4 autonomous driving system**, Yonhap reported. The deal was signed in Seoul on the sidelines of Nvidia AI Day, a two-day event that opened on 9 November.

The companies plan to combine Kakao's own driving AI models with Nvidia's computing platform, and Kakao Mobility will use Nvidia Halos, Nvidia's integrated safety system, to develop and validate the technology, Yonhap said. Seoul Economic Daily reported that the system is to be applied first to Kia's purpose-built vehicles. Level 4 means a car drives itself without a driver within set conditions and areas.

In a keynote at the same event, Nvidia's robotics vice-president Deepu Talla described work with **Hyundai, Doosan Robotics, LG Electronics and SK hynix** on factory digital twins, robot polishing and appliance-assembly robots, Chosun Biz reported. Nvidia said AI agents can cut the time to build robot simulation environments from several months to about a week.

**Analysis — why it matters:** *Analysis: the Seoul event went ahead as planned on the morning after Nvidia's board announced its investigation of Huang, and it shows how much of Nvidia's growth pitch now rests on robotics and self-driving partners outside the data centre.*

**Caveat:** A memorandum of understanding is not a binding contract, and no financial terms or launch dates were disclosed. The simulation time saving is **Nvidia's claim**. Chosun Biz says its English article was machine-translated.

### Op-eds attacking Waymo in several US newspapers were largely AI-written, according to a detection tool used by Semafor

*Industry & policy · New*

**Published:** 9 Nov 2026, 03:06 CET

Opinion pieces placed in local US newspapers arguing against the robotaxi company Waymo were written with significant AI input, and none disclosed it, Semafor reported on Sunday evening US time. The AI-detection service **Pangram** scored pieces in the Dallas Morning News, San Francisco Chronicle, New York Daily News, San Diego Union-Tribune and Times of San Diego as between **68% and 95% AI-written**, according to Semafor.

Ira Goldstein, former head of New York's Black Car Fund, told Semafor that a public relations firm drafted his Daily News op-ed and that he edited it and stands by every word; he said the firm later dropped the campaign to work for Waymo. A spokesperson for the Dallas lawyer Amy Witherite said her communications firm sometimes uses AI for initial drafts, which are then reviewed and approved by her.

Editors gave different answers: the Dallas Morning News said it does not accept pieces generated entirely by AI, the Chronicle said it asks writers to disclose AI use, and the Daily News said it aims to tighten its guardrails, citing its lawsuit against Microsoft and OpenAI.

**Analysis — why it matters:** *Analysis: the fight over robotaxis is now partly waged with **undisclosed machine-drafted opinion**, and newspapers have no shared rule on disclosure, which leaves readers unable to tell lobbying copy from personal argument.*

**Caveat:** The percentages are **probabilistic estimates from one commercial detector**, not proof of authorship, and the authors say they reviewed and approved the texts. The op-eds themselves were published between January and October 2026; only Semafor's finding is new.

**Sources:**

1. [Semafor](<https://www.semafor.com/article/11/08/2026/anti-waymo-campaign-relied-on-op-eds-written-with-ai>) — reporting. Checked: 9 Nov 2026, 06:06 CET
