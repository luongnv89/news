### Anthropic launches Claude Ship, a set of agents that review pull requests, fix them and approve the ones a team's policy allows

*Coding & agents · New*

**Published:** 23 Nov 2026

Anthropic announced **Claude Ship** on Monday, a suite of agents that takes a GitHub pull request through review, fixes and an approval decision. It combines Code Review, which is already generally available, with two new agents **in beta**: PR Steward and Claude Approvals.

PR Steward deals with failing CI checks, merge conflicts and review feedback, and after every push comments with what it changed and how to undo it; Anthropic says it is designed not to approve or merge. Claude Approvals applies an approval policy that the team writes as a file in the repository, and can **approve routine changes on its own** if the policy allows it or require human sign-off. Anthropic recommends running it in observe-only mode first.

Claude Ship is available on **Claude Team and Enterprise plans** for GitHub repositories, but not to organisations using Zero Data Retention, the HIPAA configuration or customer-managed encryption keys. Teams can set a monthly spend limit, and eligible Enterprise organisations can claim at least 10,000 dollars in Claude Ship credits until 15 January 2027.

Anthropic says that on the Claude Code team, where Claude's review is one layer of a broader process, merged pull requests more than doubled from June to September while the share reverted fell by more than 40%.

**Analysis — why it matters:** *Analysis: approval is the last step a person usually keeps; Claude Ship lets a written **policy file decide when it can be skipped**.*

**Caveat:** The merge and revert figures are **Anthropic's own internal numbers**, not an independent measurement. PR Steward and Claude Approvals are beta products.

### OpenAI and Cisco open-source Shirudo, a runtime that loads AI-generated eBPF shields to protect running Linux systems until a patch lands

*Coding & agents · New*

**Published:** 23 Nov 2026, 22:20 CET

OpenAI and Cisco released **Shirudo** on Monday, an open-source runtime that lets operators protect running Linux systems while they wait to test and roll out a patch. It uses **eBPF**, which runs small programs at specific points inside the operating system, to block the code path a vulnerability exposes without restarting the service.

In OpenAI's example, a shield generated with its **Codex** agent added an input check to Java's Base64 decoder and was attached to a running Elasticsearch process, which then rejected an exploit attempt while it kept running. OpenAI calls this a controlled test.

Operators approve each shield before it is enforced and can watch its effect first. Shields are cryptographically signed and verified before loading, and a **Trusted Platform Module** keeps a verifiable record of protections being loaded or removed. Shirudo is licensed under BSD 2-Clause, with its BPF programs dual-licensed under BSD and GPL.

The Register notes that Cisco and OpenAI pitch the tool against a flood of more than 100,000 software flaws reported this year, many found after AI bug-finders arrived.

**Analysis — why it matters:** *Analysis: AI-found bugs are arriving faster than teams can patch; Shirudo offers **temporary, signed blocks** in place of a reboot.*

**Caveat:** The Elasticsearch result is a **single vendor demonstration**. Commenters note that the setup expects measured boot and a TPM, which not every fleet has.

### Anthropic publishes a Claude-written paper and Lean proof claiming VP is not equal to VNP over every field, unchecked by any human mathematician

*Research · New*

**Published:** 23 Nov 2026, 21:04 CET

Anthropic added a folder to its public formal-math repository on Monday evening with a paper titled 'VP ≠ VNP over every field' and its **Lean 4 formalisation**. VP and VNP are the algebraic analogues of P and NP that Leslie Valiant introduced in 1979.

The README says that for every field, circuits for the Hamiltonian cycle polynomial, and for the permanent outside characteristic 2, need **more than m^(c (log m)^(1/3)) gates** for all large m, and that the same holds for approximating circuits. It states plainly that this does **not settle P vs NP**.

According to the README, the result was discovered by **an internal research Claude model**, both the paper and the Lean code were written by AI models, and **no human mathematician has checked the proof**. Lean checks the proofs, but the README warns that no machine checks that the statement file says what is claimed; the repository's CI re-checks the proofs with a second, independently written kernel.

The release comes days after the Lean-checked Riemann hypothesis proof by Anthropic's Levent Alpöge and Claude, which earlier editions covered. On Hacker News the post drew 299 points within hours.

**Analysis — why it matters:** *Analysis: a second headline result in a week puts the weight on **machine-checked statements** while human readers catch up.*

**Caveat:** This is a **research artifact, not peer-reviewed**, and the paper says it has not analysed how the result relates to the known relativisation and algebrisation barriers. Whether the Lean statement matches the claim needs human review.

### UPDATE: Commerce adds Megaspeed and six other Asian firms to the Entity List, as Jensen Huang is set to testify to the Senate Banking Committee on 3 December

*Industry & policy · Updated*

**Published:** 23 Nov 2026, 22:15 CET

Update to yesterday afternoon's edition, which reported Nvidia's board reinstating Jensen Huang as chief executive. The new development: on Monday evening the Commerce Department's Bureau of Industry and Security filed a rule adding **seven entities to the Entity List**, among them Megaspeed International in Singapore, the company where Alice Huang was an executive, and its Malaysian and Thai arms.

The rule also lists MSFusion and Speedmatrix in Malaysia and Apex Enterprise Solutions and Swiftdata in Singapore. It cites the **significant risk of diversion of advanced computing commodities** to unauthorised end users; exports of all items subject to US export rules now need a licence, reviewed under a **presumption of denial**. It went on public inspection on 23 November and is due in the Federal Register on 25 November.

A BIS spokesman told Semafor that it is now also unlawful to export advanced chips to Megaspeed even if they are made outside the United States. Semafor reports that Treasury and other agencies had backed the listing for months.

Huang is scheduled to testify before the **Senate Banking Committee on 3 December**, according to a notice on the committee's website reported by TechCrunch, after closed-door appearances before three House committees on 1 December. Semafor also reported that President Trump dialled into the Nvidia board meeting to push for Huang's return; the White House said he was 'totally involved'.

**Analysis — why it matters:** *Analysis: Washington is reinstating Huang and blacklisting the company at the centre of the scandal **on the same day**.*

**Caveat:** The Entity List rule names no wrongdoing by Nvidia. The Justice Department investigation of Megaspeed is **not concluded**, and Megaspeed has denied diverting chips. The board-call account comes from Semafor's sources.

### Virginia and Oregon sue SpaceX, X and xAI, alleging they falsely promised safeguards while Grok generated sexualised images of real people and children

*Industry & policy · New*

**Published:** 23 Nov 2026, 16:28 CET

The attorneys general of **Virginia and Oregon** filed separate but coordinated lawsuits on Monday against SpaceX and its X and xAI businesses (xAI is now called SpaceX Super Intelligence) over Grok's generation of non-consensual intimate images, other sexualised images and child sexual abuse material.

Virginia's complaint, filed in the Circuit Court for the City of Richmond, is brought under the state's **Consumer Protection Act**. It alleges the companies misrepresented that Grok and X prohibited and removed such content, marketed a 'spicy' mode and declined industry-standard safeguards. Oregon's suit was filed in Multnomah County Circuit Court.

Both offices cite a Center for Countering Digital Harm estimate that Grok created and posted about **three million sexualised images** on X between 29 December 2025 and 8 January 2026, including about 23,000 that appeared to depict children. Virginia seeks an injunction, restitution, disgorgement and civil penalties of up to 2,500 dollars per wilful violation.

The Verge reports that X's Safety team said after the filings that it takes appropriate measures against users who generate sexual content involving minors.

**Analysis — why it matters:** *Analysis: the states attack **safety claims as consumer deception**, a route that does not depend on new AI law.*

**Caveat:** These are **allegations in complaints**, not findings. The image counts are a CCDH estimate quoted in the filings.
