UPDATE: A US memo alleges Nvidia's Jensen Huang had a romantic relationship with a suspected Chinese intelligence recruit, the Financial Times reports

Published: 7 Nov 2026

Update to the 1 November morning edition, which carried Donald Trump's first comment on the New York Times report about Alice Huang. The new development, first reported on 7 November: the Financial Times says a memo that US officials wrote earlier this year, based on intelligence, alleges that Jensen Huang was romantically involved with Alice Huang, an executive at the Singapore-based data-centre company Megaspeed, according to accounts of the FT report by Kurdistan 24 and Chosun Ilbo. The people told the FT that US intelligence believes she was recruited by China's Ministry of State Security.

TechCrunch reported that the New York Times was first to report the memo, which it says claims Alice Huang was recruited to spy on Jensen Huang, and that the FT added the claim of a romantic relationship. According to Kurdistan 24's account of the FT report, the memo was shared with a small group of senior US officials shortly before Trump's May summit in Beijing. No charges have been announced, and the memo has not been published.

Nvidia, the White House and the CIA declined to comment on the memo or the relationship, Chosun Ilbo reported. Nvidia has said that Jensen Huang meets thousands of customer executives a year and that its customers receive ordinary business information, not sensitive trade secrets or national-security data. China's foreign ministry called the earlier allegations baseless speculation.

Also new to this journal since the last update: on 5 November the Republican chairs of the House Foreign Affairs, China and Intelligence committees, Brian Mast, John Moolenaar and Rick Crawford, published a letter asking Huang to give their committees a joint closed-door briefing on 18 November on his interactions with Alice Huang and on Nvidia's protections against foreign espionage. They asked him to accept that date or propose another by 11 November.

Analysis — why it matters. Analysis: the allegations now concern the chief executive of the company whose China sales depend on US export licences, and his answers to Congress could shape how closely those licences are reviewed.

Caveat. The Financial Times article is paywalled and was not read; its content here comes from Kurdistan 24's and Chosun Ilbo's summaries and TechCrunch's account. The memo is unpublished, its claims come from anonymous people describing US intelligence, and none of it has been tested in any proceeding.

Elvis Presley's rights owners and Eminem's publisher sue Suno, testing Tennessee's ELVIS Act against an AI voice-cloning tool

Published: 7 Nov 2026, 16:51 CET

Elvis Presley Enterprises, the Authentic Brands Group unit that controls Presley's intellectual property, and Eight Mile Style, which publishes many of Eminem's songs, filed separate federal lawsuits against the AI music generator Suno in San Francisco on Friday 6 November, US time, Eight Mile Style late that night. The cases were first reported on Saturday 7 November.

The Presley complaint, 51 pages according to Variety, accuses Suno of copyright infringement and of breaking Tennessee's ELVIS Act, the 2024 law against unauthorised AI cloning of voices and likenesses, by letting users generate songs that mimic Presley's voice. Billboard says it is one of the first major cases under that law, which also allows suits against companies that supply cloning tools. The complaint calls Suno's statements that it blocks artist names in prompts unequivocally false and links to videos of Presley-like output.

Eight Mile Style says Suno trained on Eminem songs it controls, including "Lose Yourself" and "Stan", and seeks more than $41 million: $150,000 for each of 269 songs, or $40.4 million, plus $672,000 for allegedly removing copyright information embedded in the songs, the Detroit Free Press reported.

A Suno spokeswoman told Billboard that Suno exists "to help people create new, original music, not to trade on anyone's name or likeness", that it does not allow prompts for specific artists' names and songs, and that it screens uploaded audio and lyrics with third-party providers. The suits add to cases against Suno from Universal Music Group and Sony Music, the publishers Round Hill and Reservoir, and musicians led by Jason Isbell; Warner Music Group settled with Suno last year.

Analysis — why it matters. Analysis: the ELVIS Act claim targets the maker of the tool rather than its users; if it survives, safeguards against voice cloning become a legal duty for AI music companies rather than a product choice.

Caveat. These are a plaintiff's allegations; Suno has not yet answered either complaint in court, and the damages are what the plaintiffs seek, not an award. Suno has argued in earlier cases that its training is fair use.

Industry & policy New

Meta's strategy chief Dave Wehner, its finance chief until 2022, leaves to become CFO of inference start-up Fireworks AI

Published: 7 Nov 2026

Dave Wehner, Meta's chief strategy officer and its chief financial officer from 2014 to 2022, is leaving the company to become chief financial officer of Fireworks AI, a platform that lets businesses customise and run open models, CNBC reported on 7 November. Wehner announced the move on X on Friday; The Information also reported it.

Fireworks, founded in 2022 by former Meta and Google employees and backed by Nvidia and Sequoia Capital, was valued at $17.5 billion in a Series D round in July. CNBC cites Business Insider as reporting early talks to raise again at a possible $30 billion to $40 billion valuation. Fireworks chief executive Lin Qiao told CNBC that Wehner will scale the company's finances and operations, and Meta's chief operating officer Javier Olivan praised his 14 years at the company.

Neither company has given a start date or said who will take over Wehner's duties at Meta, MLQ reported.

Analysis — why it matters. Analysis: inference providers are now hiring the finance chiefs of the largest technology companies, a sign of how much capital and senior talent is moving into the layer that serves AI models.

Caveat. The new funding talks and their valuation range are Business Insider's report as cited by CNBC; Fireworks has not announced a round.
