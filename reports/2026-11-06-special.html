Industry & policy New

Business AI spending barely grew in August and September even as usage rose, Ramp's economist tells TechCrunch

Published: 6 Nov 2026, 16:54 CET

Spending by US businesses on AI model tokens recorded two straight months of virtually no growth in August and September, even as token volumes rose, Ramp lead economist Ara Kharazian told TechCrunch in an article published at 15:54 UTC on Friday. Ramp, a corporate card and expense company, tracks AI payments by more than 85,000 American businesses for its Ramp AI Index.

Ramp's own October update, published on 14 October, says the top 1% of firms paying for AI spent $9,568 per employee per month in September, 3.1% below their July high of $9,873, while overall paid AI adoption reached 55.9% of businesses on Ramp. The median firm paying for AI spent $15.70 per employee per month.

New in the interview: Kharazian said token spend re-accelerated 8.9% from 23 September to 23 October, but that the headwinds remain strong. He blamed price cuts by OpenAI and Anthropic and thriftier customers rather than weaker demand. "Almost the entire spend on AI is from venture-backed, high-growth tech companies," he told TechCrunch, adding: "The era of hypergrowth may be over."

TechCrunch also reported that Anthropic said in a recent filing that its price cuts reduced revenue growth by about $600 million; that filing was not read for this edition.

Analysis — why it matters. Analysis: Ramp's card data are one of the few outside measures of what businesses actually pay OpenAI and Anthropic. If falling prices keep spending flat while usage grows, revenue growth has to come from new kinds of use, not from the same heavy spenders, which bears directly on the valuations discussed for Anthropic's IPO.

Caveat. Ramp's sample covers its own customers, and Ramp says its top-1% estimate is volatile and subject to revision. The October 8.9% figure comes from the TechCrunch interview and was not found in a published Ramp index update; most September figures were first published on 14 October.

Industry & policy New

White House adviser Hassett says ballot defeats will not slow the data centres planned for the next two years

Published: 6 Nov 2026, 16:55 CET

National Economic Council Director Kevin Hassett said on Friday that he expects data-centre growth to continue as projected despite the Democratic sweep in Tuesday's elections, CNBC reported at 15:55 UTC. Speaking on CNBC's Squawk on the Street, he said: "right now, the data centers scheduled over the next couple of years aren't affected by these ballot initiatives."

In at least 46 of at least 50 data-centre measures on 2026 ballots, majorities voted to restrict the facilities, according to Ballotpedia's tally as cited by CNBC. A CNBC Voter Poll found that 56% of midterm voters called data centres a crucial or major issue. Hassett blamed Thursday's tech-led market fall on uncertainty about what the political change means for data centres.

President Trump wrote on Truth Social on Friday that he is "100% in favor of Super Intelligence (SI)" and will work with states that want data centres, CNBC reported.

Analysis — why it matters. Analysis: The administration is signalling that it will keep backing the build-out wherever states allow it, while Democrats prepare data-centre and AI bills for January, as this afternoon's edition reported. The projects already scheduled drive the chip and power orders behind the AI trade.

Caveat. Hassett's view is an official's forecast, not a projection backed by published data. The Ballotpedia count was read only through CNBC, not on Ballotpedia itself.
